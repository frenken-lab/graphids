## Current position

We have a fast, compact source-validation candidate with strong aggregate detection
and a useful payload head. The main problem is uneven performance across attacks
and vehicles. Per-ID timing gap is the strongest of the eleven matched fits, but
small Silverado attack groups remain weak. The next work should target those
failures and confirm repeatability before expanding the architecture.

The active model is raw-frame graph-Mamba with normalized per-ID gap, raw elapsed
and bus timing, one stream, ordinary BCE, and the joint normal-payload objective.
It has approximately 624,000 trainable parameters. The weaker feature branches
and standing ablation generators have been retired; their completed artifacts
and frozen source remain available. All 15 jobs in the matched suite completed
successfully: eleven full fits, two CUDA gates and two reports. At report capture,
no Ascend jobs for this user were running or queued.

This is a dated evidence snapshot. The [implementation plan](../../MODEL_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md)
tracks future work; the [event-model reference](../reference/graph-mamba-events.md)
describes the maintained implementation.

Across all epochs, training phases total 550.27 seconds and
validation batch phases total 424.61 seconds. The remainder of the
1,328-second job includes startup, data/normalizer preparation, exact metric
finalization, checkpointing, reporting and shutdown. The phase timer ends before

not isolate their individual costs.

There are 213,167,525 training-frame exposures and 318,974,125 validation-frame
exposures over the fit. Packed validation values alone occupy approximately
14.26 GiB for the 25 epochs, plus indices and checkpoints. Artifact storage and
exact metric computation are material parts of the experiment workload.

### Limits of the speed claim

- The measured full fit used the earlier frozen implementation. Current code
  `68f0fe6` has checkpoint compatibility and CUDA correctness evidence,
  but has not completed another full 25-epoch timing run after cleanup.
- The cleanup A100 gate scored only 320 bounded source rows with 8-event chunks.
  Its roughly 619 frames/s result measures tiny-workload overhead and must not be
  substituted for the full-fit production-geometry rate.
- Older 237k/244k/344k measurements used different source/code/budgets. They are
  historical measurements, not directly comparable speedup controls for this run.
- Batch throughput does not establish online frame-to-score latency. We have no
  p50/p95 arrival-to-alert measurement or timer-driven silence detector. A causal
  model can still have deployment batching delay.
- Sampled free GPU memory does not establish that more streams or a larger model
  will train faster. Earlier stream sweeps and these complete-job times provide
  no reason to widen the default merely to fill VRAM.

## Strengths

1. **Strong tested timing cue.** Same-ID gap is the best individual intervention
   on this source cohort; DoS AP is about 0.98 on both vehicles.
2. **Useful payload learning.** Aggregate payload AP 0.7344/AUC 0.9953 is a marked
   improvement over the old baseline's weak payload ranking. It remains available
   for diagnostics without forcing an untested fusion rule.
3. **Manageable training cost.** A complete 25-epoch quality run fits in roughly
   22 minutes on one A100, with about 624k parameters and modest sampled memory.
4. **Explicit causal and coverage contracts.** Raw rows and available fields are
   retained; streams preserve chronology, forecast heads exclude current payload,
   normalizers fit normal training data only, and tails/unknown labels are tracked.
5. **Verified implementation and reproducible evidence.** The cleanup gate passed
   147 tests on A100 and bitwise baseline/winner checkpoint parity on six synthetic
   frames across two chunks. Subsequent reporting changes pass the 125-test CPU
   suite, including ten focused report/suite tests. Source/config/manifest hashes,
   dependency versions, score artifacts and Slurm receipts are retained.
6. **Reduced maintenance burden.** One maintained candidate and three jobs per
   new suite replace eleven standing fits. Reports expose recording macro AP,
   weakest captures, per-head scores and support differences.

## Weaknesses and open questions

1. **Aggregate metrics hide rare-group failures.** Validation DoS supplies 87.38%
   of positives; recording macro AP is only 0.5046. Silverado force-neutral and
   double have zero recall at 0.5; fuzzing recall is 1.16%. RPM is also weak.
2. **Training is heavily skewed.** DoS supplies 93.23% of training positives.
   A global positive-class weight cannot balance the relative attack families.
3. **Very limited independent data.** Ten training captures include only two
   verified-normal captures. One seed, two source vehicles, and repeated selection
   on the same validation recordings limit generalization claims.
4. **Unstable learning curve.** Aggregate AP at epochs 23/24/25 is approximately
   0.9041 / 0.7261 / 0.9257. The cause is not isolated; convergence and a benefit
   from extra epochs are not established.
5. **Local history is only partly learned.** The model has explicit same-ID gap
   and a graph of preceding global-encoder states. It does not yet have a shared
   recurrent encoder over each ID's own payload sequence.
6. **Operating-point evaluation is unfinished.** Separate source calibration and
   confirmation roles/captures, alert aggregation, false alerts/hour, and trusted
   incident intervals are still missing from the completed evaluation.
7. **Historical checkpoint caveat.** Seven retired arms have stale `last.ckpt`
   files from the old callback. All best-AP checkpoints and sidecars verified;
   the winner's epoch-25/step-3300 checkpoint is current. The callback is fixed
   and regression-tested in maintained code.
8. **Some speed costs remain unprofiled for this candidate.** Metric finalization,
   storage, startup and serving latency deserve direct measurements. Ray's
   monitoring warnings did not prevent completed fits but add operational noise.

## Recommended next work

1. Diagnose the saved winner's weak captures: support rates, score distributions,
   pooled non-DoS AP and classifier/payload disagreement, preserving all rows.
2. Freeze calibration/confirmation grouping and repeat the retained recipe with
   seeds 43 and 44 before promoting another mechanism.
3. Test one cheap recording/group-balanced frozen-encoder classifier intervention,
   with weights normalized using training-wide information rather than canceled
   within single-recording optimizer windows.
4. If evidence points to missing representation capacity, add a small shared
   causal per-ID recurrent state alongside the retained global model.
5. Profile complete epochs and metric finalization; benchmark online latency
   separately. Treat throughput optimization and quality acceptance as different
   measurements.

The next priority is improving coverage of weak groups while retaining the
current speed and correctness evidence. Bigger models, more streams, more epochs
and score fusion need their own controlled justification.
